Elden Ring Nightreign: The Forsaken Hollows — Complete DLC Guide

Released December 4, 2025   Patch 1.03.2   ~15 min read

1. What Is The Forsaken Hollows DLC?

Seven months after launch, FromSoftware dropped The Forsaken Hollows — Nightreign's first paid DLC — on December 4, 2025. It is not a side-map curiosity. It is the most significant content injection the game has received: two new Nightfarers with genuinely novel playstyles, a sprawling subterranean map called The Great Hollow, a brutal new Expedition mode, and a final boss with dual health bars that will make you question every life choice that led you there.

Price & Value: The DLC is $14.99 / €14.99 standalone. If you are actively playing Nightreign in 2026 — especially after Patch 1.03.2 revived the struggling base roster — this is the best $15 you will spend on the game. Two endgame-viable characters, three new bosses, a full map, and an Expedition mode that reshapes how runs play out.

The DLC introduces a new threat called Abominations — entities distinct from the Nightlords, born from cursed crystals deep beneath Limveld. These things are gnarly, unpredictable, and genuinely terrifying the first time one lumbers out of the darkness. The Scholar and Undertaker are built specifically to counter them, and mastering both characters fundamentally changes how much of Nightreign's endgame feels.

From a 2026 perspective — with Bandai Namco confirming the content scope and months of player data behind us — yes, the DLC is absolutely worth buying. The Scholar alone is the most interesting support character in the game, and the Undertaker's Trance-meets-Loathsome-Hex loop is some of the most satisfying combat design FromSoft has shipped since Sekiro's deflection system.

Here is the thing nobody told me before I bought it: the DLC doesn't just add content — it fixes the meta. Before Patch 1.03.2, characters like Raider and Revenant were borderline grief-picks in higher-difficulty Expeditions. Then the patch buffed six Nightfarers into relevance, and suddenly we had a real roster. The DLC characters now slot into a balanced cast where every pick feels intentional rather than "I guess I'll play Wylder again."

2. How to Unlock Scholar & Undertaker

If you have been playing Nightreign for a while, you may already meet the unlock conditions without realizing it. The requirements are refreshingly straightforward compared to some of the obtuse unlock chains FromSoft is infamous for:

1
Complete the Tricephalos Expedition. This means beating Gladius, the Beast of Night on Night 3. If you have not done this yet, you need to beat the base game's core Expedition first — the DLC does not unlock until you have proven you can handle a Nightlord.
2
Speak to the Iron Menial in the Roundtable Hold after your Tricephalos clear. He will grumble something cryptic about "new souls drawn to the Hollows" — pay attention, because if you skip his dialogue you will be stuck wondering why the next step does not trigger.
3
Find the Small Jar Merchant near the newly opened Great Hollow entrance. He sells the Scholar and Undertaker's starting relics (both free for the first purchase). Acquire them, and both characters appear in your Nightfarer select screen.

That is it. No secret NPC questlines that lock you out if you killed a random crab three hours ago. No "activate three invisible pressure plates while emote-dancing during a blood moon" nonsense. FromSoftware learned something from the base game's unlock criticism, and the DLC respects your time.

Once unlocked, both characters are available in all Expedition modes — including the new Dreglord Expedition, which is where they truly shine.

3. Scholar Deep Dive: The Arcane Observer

Build Identity: INT/ARC support-controller hybrid. You are not the main DPS — you are the force multiplier that makes the whole team 40% deadlier. A well-played Scholar turns Night 3 boss fights from "we wiped at 30%" to "wait, it's already dead?"

Core Stats & Loadout

StatValueNotes
Base HP220Tied for lowest in the game — you are squishy
Base FP65Solid; your abilities are FP-hungry
Base Stamina54Enough for Thrusting Sword pokes, not enough to panic-roll
Favored AttributesArcaneScales everything: Analyze duration, Communion damage share %, item power
Favored WeaponsThrusting SwordsFast pokes let you tag enemies for Analyze while staying mobile

Passive Ability: Bagcraft

Bagcraft is what makes Scholar the best item-user in the game, full stop. You get extra consumable slots, and — crucially — items level up as you use them. Throw three firebombs? The fourth applies a burn DoT and has 20% larger AoE. Chug three Crimson Flasks? The fourth also gives a small HP regen buff. At item level 3 (roughly 8-10 uses of a single item type), effects get even stronger — we are talking frostbite buildup on throwing knives, wider heal zones on warming stones, and extended duration on every buff consumable.

The real galaxy-brain play: coordinate with your team so you are the designated item user. Let the Undertaker or Wylder carry the damage flasks. You carry every utility item. By Night 3, your upgraded items are war crimes.

Character Skill: Analyze

Hold the skill button to fill a circular gauge while targeting an enemy. The fuller the gauge when you release, the stronger the effect:

  • On enemies: Applies a defense debuff (up to 25% at max gauge) and reveals their elemental weaknesses, resistances, and upcoming attack patterns for 15 seconds. Against bosses, this is practically maphack.
  • On self: Applies a stacking damage buff (up to 20% at max) and accelerated FP regen. Use this during boss phase transitions when you have a window.

Here is the hidden sauce: each unique enemy type you fully Analyze permanently strengthens your future Analyzes for that expedition run. By Night 3, if you have been diligent, your Analyzes are nearly instant and the debuff values are significantly higher. The Scholar rewards curiosity — run around scanning everything in Night 1 and 2, even trash mobs. It pays off.

Ultimate Art: Communion

Communion fires a wide arcane pulse that links all enemies within its radius. Damage dealt to any linked enemy is shared across the entire linked group. That boss with two adds? Link all three, nuke the weakest add, and watch the boss melt. It also applies a healing tether to any linked allies — every time a linked enemy takes damage, tethered allies recover a small percentage of HP.

The range on this thing is ridiculous. I have hit enemies I could not even see on screen. Use it in high-density rooms (the Great Hollow has plenty) and you will feel like a god. The cooldown is long — roughly 90 seconds — so do not waste it on single-target fights unless the boss is about to phase-change.

Real Struggle: My first Scholar run, I did not realize Analyze had a self-cast mode. I spent two full Expeditions only debuffing enemies, wondering why the "buff yourself" description was there. Read your tooltips, people. The self-buff is what makes Scholar viable in solo play — without it, your damage is tragic.

4. Undertaker Deep Dive: The Death Abbess

Build Identity: STR/FAI hyper-aggressive melee bruiser. The Undertaker does not block, does not dodge lightly, and does not care about your idea of "safe spacing." She runs directly at the problem and turns it into a red stain. If Scholar is a scalpel, Undertaker is a sledgehammer covered in barbed wire.

Core Stats & Loadout

Base FP65Used almost entirely for Trance; you will burn through it fast
Base Stamina54Tight for a STR character; Trance's stamina regen is mandatory
Favored AttributesStrength, FaithSTR scales melee damage, FAI scales Trance power and Loathsome Hex damage
Favored WeaponsHammersGreat Hammers hit like trucks and Trance cancels their slow recovery

Passive Ability: Confluence

Confluence is the Undertaker's secret weapon and the reason she breaks the action economy in half. The passive triggers in two ways:

  • Ally/Foe Trigger: When a teammate lands a critical hit or an enemy performs certain heavy attacks (indicated by a brief crimson flash on the enemy), your Ultimate gauge partially refills — letting you use Loathsome Hex far more often than the cooldown would normally allow.
  • Perfect Dodge: If you perfectly dodge an enemy grab attack (not just any attack — specifically grabs), Confluence activates and your next Loathsome Hex deals bonus damage. This is high-risk, high-reward, and landing it against Heolstor's grab is one of the most satisfying things in the game.

Character Skill: Trance

Activating Trance sends the Undertaker into a frenzied state. You attack faster, move faster, and regain stamina rapidly. The longer you keep attacking, the stronger these effects become — the buff stacks infinitely as long as you do not drop your combo. At 10+ consecutive hits, Undertaker is practically a blur. At 20+, you are playing a completely different game from everyone else.

The advanced tech: pressing Character Skill + Ultimate Art simultaneously spends your entire Ultimate gauge to supercharge Trance. This "Super Trance" grants auto-dodge — incoming attacks that would hit you are automatically avoided for the duration. This is your "I am going to facetank this boss and there is nothing you can do about it" button. The tradeoff is losing Loathsome Hex access, but against aggressive bosses like Heolstor, the auto-dodge is genuinely more valuable than one big hit.

Ultimate Art: Loathsome Hex

Loathsome Hex is pure spectacle. The Undertaker rips a grotesque bone spear from her own body (the animation is viscerally unsettling in the best FromSoft way), then launches herself across the screen at blinding speed toward the targeted enemy. Everything in the path takes damage. The impact applies heavy stagger and a curse DoT that ticks for 8 seconds.

What makes it special in the DLC meta: because Confluence can partially reset the gauge, you can fire off 3-4 Loathsome Hexes in a single boss fight if your team coordinates their critical hits well. The curse DoT stacks, and against Heolstor's dual health bars, stacking 3-4 curses during phase transitions is often the difference between a clean kill and a wipe at 5%.

My Undertaker Epiphany: I spent my first five Undertaker runs treating her like a standard STR character — dodge, punish, repeat. I was wrong. The Undertaker's entire kit screams "STOP DODGING AND START SWINGING." Once I embraced the Trance-stacking playstyle and stopped worrying about getting hit (Super Trance auto-dodge handles it), my clear times dropped by 40%. She punishes hesitation harder than any boss punishes greed.

6. Death Knight Trio Boss Guide

Location: Night 1, Dreglord Expedition — The Great Hollow Depths

The Death Knight Trio is the gateway boss of the DLC — your first major skill check in the Dreglord Expedition. Think of them as this DLC's equivalent of the base game's Demi-Human Chiefs, except all three are actually threatening and they have synergy patterns that will obliterate you if you do not respect them.

The three Knights are:

  • Vanguard Knight (Sword & Shield): Frontline tank. High physical block, slow but devastating shield bashes. Do not focus this one first. He is the distraction — the other two are the real threats.
  • Hex Knight (Staff): Ranged magic support. Casts curse bolts and a ground-targeted AoE that applies Death Blight buildup. Kill this one first. If left unchecked, the Death Blight AoE will force constant repositioning and your melee players will accomplish nothing.
  • Executioner Knight (Greatsword): High burst damage melee. Slow windups but massive hitboxes. When below 50% HP, he gains a self-buff that adds frostbite buildup to every swing. Second priority.

Strategy

Phase 1 (100%-60% all three alive): Scholar should Analyze the Hex Knight immediately — revealing his weak point (his staff arm takes 30% extra damage). Undertaker holds the Vanguard Knight's attention with Trance-fueled aggression while the third teammate (ideally Wylder or Executor) focuses the Hex Knight. Spread out. The Hex Knight's curse bolts chain between targets that are too close together.

Phase 2 (one Knight dead): The surviving Knights gain a shared damage buff ("Oath of the Fallen") — they hit ~40% harder. This is where Scholar's Communion shines: link the two remaining Knights, focus the weaker one, and the damage sharing mechanic makes short work of both. If you lost the Hex Knight first (as you should), this phase is manageable. If you lost the Vanguard first by mistake, the remaining Hex + Executioner combo is punishingly hard — consider resetting the run.

Phase 3 (one Knight remaining): Solo Executioner or solo Vanguard is straightforward — standard boss pattern recognition. Solo Hex Knight at this phase is dangerous because he spams the Death Blight AoE with reduced cooldown; Undertaker's Super Trance auto-dodge really carries here.

7. Heolstor the Nightlord — Final Boss Guide

Location: Night 3, Dreglord Expedition — The Crystal Throne

Heolstor is the DLC's Nightlord and the hardest boss in all of Nightreign as of 2026. Full stop. He has two complete health bars with entirely different movesets, a grab that one-shots most builds, and an arena hazard (cursed crystal growths) that limits your safe space as the fight progresses. I died to this thing 14 times before my first clear. It is magnificent.

Health Bar 1: Heolstor, Keeper of the Deep

This form is a massive, slow-moving crystalline entity. Attacks are telegraphed but hitbox-wide:

  • Crystal Sweep: Wide horizontal arc. Roll toward Heolstor, not away — the hitbox extends further than the visual suggests.
  • Abyssal Geyser: Three targeted ground eruptions in sequence. The third always tracks your current position — delay your final dodge by half a beat.
  • Grasp of the Hollows: The grab. A long windup, then Heolstor lunges forward with both arms. If caught, you take massive damage and he heals for 15% of his current HP. This is the run-ender. Perfect-dodging this with Undertaker's Confluence is the intended counter.
  • Crystal Spire Summon: At 70% and 40%, Heolstor summons crystal growths around the arena that persist into the second health bar. These deal chip damage on contact and reduce your available arena space. Destroy them when safe, but do not tunnel-vision — the boss is still attacking.

Scholar-specific tip: Analyzed Heolstor reveals that his left rear flank (behind his crystalline shoulder) takes 35% extra damage. Position your team accordingly. Communion is less effective here since it is a single-target fight, but the self-buff mode of Analyze makes Scholar a competent secondary DPS in this phase.

Undertaker-specific tip: This is the fight where Confluence's perfect-dodge trigger on grabs truly shines. Heolstor's grab has a distinct audio cue — a deep crystalline grinding sound — that makes the dodge timing learnable. Save your Super Trance for the Crystal Spire summon windows so you can ignore the chip damage and focus on destroying the spires while maintaining DPS.

Health Bar 2: Heolstor, Night Aspect

Once the first health bar drops, Heolstor sheds his crystalline shell and emerges as a faster, more aggressive entity wreathed in dark energy. All crystal spires from Phase 1 remain — you are now fighting in a minefield.

  • Nightstep: A rapid dash-punish combo. Heolstor teleports behind a random player and immediately attacks. Watch for the purple flash — that is your half-second warning.
  • Veil of Sorrow: An arena-wide darkness pulse that deals percentage-based HP damage (25% of current HP) and applies a stacking defense debuff. This cannot be dodged; you must use the crystal spires as cover. Stand behind a spire when the cast bar fills.
  • Hollow Requiem (Enrage at 25%): Heolstor channels for 6 seconds, then unleashes a massive AoE that deals lethal damage to anyone not standing behind cover. All remaining crystal spires in the arena explode after the cast, so you lose your cover. After this, the fight becomes a pure DPS race — no more mechanics, just raw aggression from the boss.

Team composition for first clear: Scholar + Undertaker + Wylder. Scholar keeps Veil of Sorrow debuffs manageable with Communion heals and item support. Undertaker saves Super Trance for Hollow Requiem — the auto-dodge lets her keep attacking while everyone else scrambles. Wylder provides consistent all-range damage and can revive at range with Restage. This is the comp I cleared with after the aforementioned 14 deaths, and it felt earned.

Heolstor Reward: First clear drops the "Crystalheart Remembrance" — exchangeable for the Scholar's Arcane Crown (headpiece, +15% Analyze speed) or the Undertaker's Abbess Mantle (chest, +2 Confluence triggers per fight). Both are best-in-slot for their respective characters. You will need to beat Heolstor twice to get both.

8. Dreglord Expedition Mode Overview

The Dreglord Expedition is the DLC's new game mode and it is significantly harder than the base Tricephalos Expedition. If the base game's Expedition is roughly equivalent to a standard Souls run, Dreglord is more like a challenge run with permanent modifiers.

What Makes It Different

  • The Great Hollow Map: A massive subterranean zone with verticality — multi-level ruins, crystal bridges, and dark tunnels. Navigation matters. Getting lost (and you will, at first) means fewer camps cleared before the Night Lord fight.
  • Abomination Encounters: Random roaming mini-bosses that patrol the map. They are optional but drop high-tier relics and weapons. Ignoring them means a weaker final build; fighting them means risking a wipe before Night 3. Risk assessment is the core tension of this mode.
  • Cursed Crystal Zones: Certain areas apply a stacking curse debuff that reduces max HP while you stand in them. These zones expand as the Nights progress, shrinking safe space. By Night 3, roughly 40% of the map is cursed. Route planning between safe zones becomes essential.
  • Boss Order: Night 1 = Death Knight Trio. Night 2 = randomized Abomination lord (three possible variants: Crystalfang Wyrm, Hollow Saint, or Abyssal Warden — all three have distinct movesets, so study up). Night 3 = Heolstor.

Recommended Approach

For your first Dreglord clear, run Scholar + Undertaker + Guardian. Scholar handles item scaling and boss debuffs, Undertaker provides the burst damage, and Guardian (buffed significantly in 1.03.2) keeps both squishy DLC characters alive with improved damage negation and Guard Boost. The Guardian's tanking lets Scholar stand still long enough to land full-gauge Analyzes, which is the single biggest force multiplier in the DLC.

Do not try to full-clear the map on your first attempts. Aim for 6-8 camps per Night, prioritize Abominations that drop weapon upgrades, and always clear at least one crystal spire cluster before the Heolstor fight — entering Phase 2 with a cluttered arena is a death sentence.

9. DLC in the Current Meta (Patch 1.03.2)

Patch 1.03.2 dropped in early 2026 and transformed the meta. The headline changes — buffs to Raider's attack speed, Guardian's damage negation, Revenant's spirit damage, and Executor's near-everything — mean the base roster is no longer a liability next to the DLC characters. Here is the current competitive landscape as of July 2026:

S
Scholar, Undertaker, Wylder — The DLC duo plus the eternal all-rounder. Scholar's utility and Undertaker's burst are unmatched, and Wylder remains the safest pick for any comp. If you see these three on a team, the run is highly likely to clear.
A
Executor, Duchess — Executor's 1.03.2 buffs turned a niche parry character into a legitimate DPS threat. Duchess' debuff stacking synergizes beautifully with Scholar's Analyze for boss-melting combos. Both are strong picks that require more execution than S-tiers.
B
Guardian, Revenant, Raider — All three are much better post-1.03.2 than they were at launch. Guardian is now a legitimate tank, Revenant's spirits actually do damage, and Raider's faster swings make him usable. They are still outclassed by the S/A tiers in coordinated play, but entirely viable in random matchmaking.

What is fascinating from a meta perspective: the DLC characters did not power-creep the base roster into irrelevance. Instead, they created new team compositions that did not exist before. Scholar/Undertaker/Wylder is the "meta" comp, but Scholar/Duchess/Executor (double debuff stacking) and Undertaker/Guardian/Raider (triple-melee aggression) are both tournament-viable. The DLC expanded the game's strategic depth rather than flattening it. That is a rare achievement in live-service gaming, and FromSoftware deserves credit for getting it right. IGN's character breakdown confirms the community consensus: these are balanced, well-designed additions that make the game better, not just bigger.

10. Conclusion: Should You Buy The Forsaken Hollows?

Yes.

If you want the short answer, there it is. If you want the nuanced answer — here is the breakdown:

Buy it if: You are actively playing Nightreign, enjoy endgame Expeditions, want two genuinely novel playstyles to master, or care about the meta. The Scholar is the first support character in a FromSoft game that feels good to play rather than like a self-imposed challenge. The Undertaker's Trance system is some of the most satisfying combat design in the studio's catalog. The Heolstor fight is an all-timer boss encounter. The new map is beautiful and the cursed-crystal mechanic adds real strategic depth to Expedition routing.

Skip it if: You bounced off Nightreign's core loop, never cleared the Tricephalos Expedition, or are firmly in the "I only play single-player" camp. The DLC does not fundamentally change what Nightreign is — it makes Nightreign better at being Nightreign. If you did not like the base game, the DLC will not convert you.

At $14.99, The Forsaken Hollows delivers roughly 15-20 hours of meaningful new content — and if you are the type to farm Heolstor for both Remembrance rewards or run Dreglord Expeditions for leaderboard times, that number can easily triple. In a world where $15 buys you a single cosmetic skin in most games, getting two S-tier characters, three bosses, a full map, and a new game mode is exceptional value.

The Scholar and Undertaker are not just additions to the roster — they are the reason I personally came back to Nightreign after a three-month break. There is something deeply satisfying about watching your team demolish a boss because your Analyze revealed the weak point, or chain-casting Loathsome Hex three times in a row because Confluence procced perfectly. These characters make Nightreign feel new again, and in 2026, that is the highest compliment I can give a DLC.
